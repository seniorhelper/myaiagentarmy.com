Where the hours go

Agency margins leak through the work clients never see

Marketing, creative, digital and SEO agencies sell strategy and craft. Yet a surprising share of every account's hours goes to assembling reports, onboarding paperwork, content scheduling and status emails. AI agents for agencies are designed to absorb that operational layer so your strategists and creatives stay on the work clients hired you for.

We know this world from the inside. Eye To Ad Media has run as a Denver-headquartered marketing agency since 2012 and tests tactics on its own companies before rolling them out for clients. The agents we build come out of the same reality you live in: too many accounts, too many dashboards, too many "quick questions."

Monthly reporting

Pulling numbers from analytics, ads, search and social platforms, pasting them into a template and writing commentary, multiplied by every client.

Client onboarding

Collecting logins, brand assets, access permissions and questionnaires, then following up when half of it never arrives.

Content operations

Briefs, first drafts, approval rounds, scheduling and reposting across channels and clients.

Account inbox

Status checks, small requests and "can you send that again" messages that interrupt deep work all day.

Building for your clients

How a client agent project typically runs

When an agency brings us in for a client, the goal is simple: your client gets a well-built agent, and you stay the trusted advisor. A typical engagement looks like this:

  1. You identify the opportunity. Maybe a client keeps missing calls, or their review count stalls. You already know their business better than anyone.
  2. Joint Blueprint. We run the $750 Agent Blueprint with you and, where useful, your client, capturing tools, rules, approvals and who supports the agent after launch.
  3. Written scope. Development is quoted from our published starting points, and deployment into the client's phone system, CRM or inbox gets its own written scope before work begins.
  4. Build and test. We develop and test the agent against real scenarios from the client's business. You review the experience before your client does.
  5. Launch and care. Done-For-You with Agent Care, Co-Pilot where your team handles deployment, or Handoff with documentation. Responsibilities are written down so nobody is guessing later.

That last point matters. An agent that answers your client's customers needs a clear owner when something changes, such as new hours, new prices or a new service. Agreeing on that up front protects your relationship. For ideas on which client agents tend to be most useful first, look at our receptionist agents; missed calls are among the most common pain points small businesses share.

Your craft, your call

What stays human at an agency

  1. Strategy. Positioning, channel mix, budgets and priorities are set by people who know the client.
  2. Creative judgment. Drafts are drafts. Brand voice, final copy and design decisions belong to your team.
  3. Client relationships. Every sensitive conversation, renewal, upsell and bad-news call is handled by a person.
  4. Spend. No agent changes ad budgets, buys media or commits client money without approval.
  5. Publishing. Client-facing content goes out only on the approval rules you set per account.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What can AI agents do for a marketing agency?

They can assemble draft client reports from connected platforms, run client onboarding requests and reminders, draft content from briefs, schedule approved social posts, monitor rankings and site health, and triage the account inbox. People approve anything client-facing.

Do you offer white label AI agents?

Agencies can bring us in to build agents for their clients. If you want to present those agents under your own brand, raise it in the Agent Blueprint and we will discuss what is workable for that engagement and put it in writing. We do not offer a self-serve white-label platform.

Will an agent publish content or change ad spend on its own?

Only within approval rules you set. Most agencies require a person to approve client-facing content, and no agent changes budgets or commits client money without human sign-off.

How do you keep client data separate?

Agents are configured with permissions per client account so each one sees only the data its job requires, and actions are logged for review. Details are designed during the Blueprint.

What do agency AI agents cost?

Scouts start from $1,500, Specialists from $3,500 and Commanders from $7,500 to build and own, or from $149, $299 and $599 per month (plus a one-time setup fee) on Subscribe-to-Own. Five or more agents get estimated volume pricing, and deployment is quoted after the $750 Blueprint.
