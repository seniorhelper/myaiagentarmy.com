Law, accounting and consulting

AI agents for firms that sell expertise by the hour

If your firm bills for judgment, every hour spent chasing a signed engagement letter or re-sending a document checklist is an hour of expertise left on the table. We build narrow AI agents for law firms, accounting practices, consultancies and other professional offices that handle intake, scheduling, document collection and reminders, and never cross into advice.

Where the hours go

The billable day is shorter than it looks

Clio's 2025 Legal Trends Report offers a sobering benchmark for legal practices, and many accountants and consultants will recognize the pattern in their own calendars.

According to Clio's benchmarks, the average law firm utilization rate in 2025 was 38%. The rest of the day disappears into work that has to get done but rarely gets billed:

  • Intake calls and forms from prospective clients, many of whom are not a fit.
  • Calendar coordination for consultations, signings and client meetings.
  • Document chasing for tax organizers, bank statements, IDs, contracts and discovery materials.
  • Reminder emails about deadlines, missing items and unpaid invoices.
  • Status questions from clients who just want to know where things stand.

The non-negotiables

Agents organize. Professionals advise.

Hard line: our agents never give legal, tax, financial or medical advice. They don't interpret a contract, estimate a refund, predict a case outcome or tell anyone what to do. When a question leans that way, the agent says a professional will follow up and routes it to the right person.

That boundary is built into the instructions, the test scenarios and the approval rules. It also matters for professional conduct obligations: in many professions, giving advice creates responsibilities that only a licensed person should take on. Your firm still sets and owns those policies; we design agents to stay inside them.

Confidentiality

Agents work inside your systems and accounts wherever possible, and see only the data their job requires.

Access controls

Role-based permissions mean the intake agent can't open client files, and the reminder agent can't read case notes.

Audit trail

Messages and actions are logged so your team can review what was sent, when and to whom.

See our data handling and approval guardrails for the full picture, and bring your firm's security requirements to the Blueprint so we can design around them.

A new matter, step by step

From first inquiry to a ready-to-review file

Notice what the agent does not do: it doesn't decide whether to take the matter, run the conflict check itself or discuss fees beyond what you publish. It gathers, organizes and schedules, then hands a person a clean file.

For a solo practitioner, that might mean waking up to two booked consultations and their intake summaries instead of six voicemails. For a multi-partner firm, it can mean each inquiry reaching the right practice group automatically, with the intake answers already attached and nothing sitting in a general inbox over the weekend. Either way, your team spends its first hour of the day reviewing prepared work rather than reconstructing it from scattered messages.

The client experience

Faster responses without sounding automated

Prospective clients often reach out at a stressful moment: a contract dispute, an IRS letter, a business decision that can't wait. When they hear nothing for a day, many simply contact the next firm. When they get a calm, clear acknowledgment and a booked consultation, they feel taken care of before they ever meet you.

That first impression is where an intake agent earns its place. It is designed to:

  • Acknowledge promptly, any hour, in language your firm approved.
  • Set expectations honestly, explaining that a professional will review the inquiry and what happens next.
  • Collect only what is needed for a conflict check and a productive first meeting, without asking for sensitive details that belong in a privileged conversation.
  • Offer real appointment times instead of a promise to call back.
  • Stay courteous when it can't help, for example when a matter is outside your practice areas, with a polite note and any referral resources you choose to share.

For existing clients, the most common call is some version of "where are we?" A status agent can answer from the milestones your team has marked complete, like "we've received your documents and your return is in review," without exposing case notes or offering opinions. Those calls stop interrupting the professionals doing the work, and clients stop wondering whether anyone remembered them.

Try this first: list the five questions your front desk answers most often. If none of them require professional judgment, they are strong candidates for an agent. Everything else stays with your people.

Responsibility

What stays human at the firm

Professional judgment is the product, so it never moves to an agent. That includes deciding whether to accept a client, clearing conflicts, setting and negotiating fees, every piece of advice and every filing, signing anything, and handling a distressed or upset client. FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can an AI agent give legal or tax advice?

No. Our agents are built never to give legal, tax, financial or medical advice. When a question moves toward advice, the agent tells the person a professional will follow up and routes it to your team.

What can AI agents do for a law firm?

They can handle after-hours intake with questions you approve, gather conflict-check details for staff, book consultations, send document checklists and secure upload links, track what is missing, and send reminders. Attorneys keep every decision about accepting matters and giving advice.

How do you protect client confidentiality?

Agents work inside your systems wherever possible, use role-based permissions so each agent sees only what its job requires, and log their actions for review. Your firm's security requirements are captured in the Agent Blueprint and designed into the build.

Do the agents work with accounting and practice management software?

Agents are designed to connect to the tools you already use, such as practice management, document management, calendars and QuickBooks. Each connection is scoped in writing after the Blueprint.
