How to Keep Your Pipes From Freezing This Winter

When ice forms inside a pipe, it creates a blockage. Water trapped between that ice and a closed faucet has nowhere to go, so the pressure climbs until the pipe splits. The usual suspects are pipes that run through exterior walls, unheated garages, crawl spaces and attics, along with outdoor hose bibs and sprinkler lines. Kitchen and bathroom sinks on outside walls are common trouble spots because the pipes sit inside a cabinet, away from the heat of the room. Open the cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm air can reach the pipes. If you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out, a pipe may be frozen. If you cannot find the frozen section, cannot reach it, or the pipe has already split, shut off the main water and call a plumber. Turning off the water quickly is the single best way to limit damage from a burst pipe.

Why Is My Water Heater Making Popping Noises?

If your water heater has started popping, rumbling or crackling, you are not imagining it. The good news is that a noisy water heater is usually not an emergency. In most tank water heaters, popping comes from sediment. On a gas water heater, the burner sits directly under the bottom of the tank. A loud bang, water leaking from the base of the tank, or hot water spraying from the temperature and pressure relief valve are signs to shut the heater off and call a plumber. A flush drains water out through the drain valve at the bottom of the tank, carrying loose sediment with it. Every tank water heater has an anode rod, a metal rod that corrodes on purpose so the tank does not. Most tank water heaters last about 8 to 12 years.
