The marketing to-do list that never shrinks

Marketing is a hundred small tasks, and they all fall to the bottom of the list

Post this week. Update the Google Business Profile. Ask last month's customers for reviews. Reply to the reviews that came in. Draft the newsletter. Check whether the site is still ranking. None of it is hard. All of it takes time you would rather spend running the business.

So it slides. The social account goes quiet for six weeks. The profile still shows last year's photos. Happy customers are never asked for a review, and the one unhappy review sits there unanswered. Not because anyone does not care, but because the work is constant and nobody's whole job.

That is the sweet spot for an AI marketing agent: steady, repeatable tasks that need to happen every week, done consistently, with a person approving anything before it goes public.

Why reviews are worth automating

Customers are willing to review you. Someone just has to ask.

BrightLocal's 2025 survey of 1,026 US adults found that 96% of consumers are open to writing a review for a business, and email is the request method they are most likely to respond to. Asking is the step that gets skipped when the week gets busy, which makes it a natural first job for an agent.

A review agent does not write reviews, buy them or filter who gets asked based on how happy they seem. It asks every eligible customer the same way, which keeps you on the right side of platform rules and keeps your reputation honest.

One week, mostly hands-off

How the marketing agents work together with you in the loop

For a typical local business, that approval sitting can take fifteen minutes on a Monday morning: scan the drafts, fix a word or two, swap a photo, approve. The rest of the week runs on its own. Review requests go out as jobs close in your CRM or orders ship from your store, so the timing matches the moment a customer is most likely to be happy and willing to share it. Replies to new reviews arrive as drafts in your inbox or team chat, each one specific to what the reviewer actually said rather than a copy-paste "thanks for your business."

The approval step is the whole design. Nothing goes public under your name until a person on your team has said yes. Over time you can choose to let low-risk items, such as a thank-you reply to a five-star review, publish after a lighter check. That choice is always yours. Stays with you

The human side of AI agents for marketing

  1. Your voice and your claims. Agents draft; you decide what your brand says. Anything involving pricing, guarantees or regulated claims gets extra review.
  2. Negative reviews. These always come to a person first. A thoughtful human reply to a complaint is one of the most visible things a business does.
  3. Spending. Agents can report on ad performance, but they do not launch campaigns or change budgets without approval.
  4. Strategy. What to say, to whom, and why. The agents carry out the plan you set.

Teaching it your voice

How we train a marketing agent to sound like you, not like a robot

The biggest worry owners have about AI-written marketing is that it will sound generic. That is a fair concern, and it is mostly a setup problem. Here is what goes into every marketing agent before its first draft reaches you.

A voice guide built from your real writing

We collect emails, posts, proposals and replies you are proud of, then write down the patterns: sentence length, words you use, words you never use, how formal you are with customers.

An approved facts sheet

Services, service areas, hours, credentials and the claims you are comfortable making. The agent drafts only from that sheet, so it does not invent offers or awards.

Your own photos and stories

Real project photos and real moments make posts worth reading. The agent asks for them, organizes them and writes around them instead of relying on stock images.

Your edits, fed back in

Every time you change a draft, the change becomes guidance for the next one. Over the first few weeks, drafts typically need fewer edits.

Think of the first month as onboarding a new marketing assistant. It gets better as it learns your preferences, and you stay in control of everything it publishes. FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What does an AI marketing agent do?

Each marketing agent handles one recurring task: drafting content in your voice, scheduling social posts, preparing Google Business Profile updates, sending review requests, drafting review replies or monitoring rankings and site health. A person approves anything before it goes public.

Will AI agents for marketing post without my approval?

Not by default. Every agent is set up with an approval step before publishing. You can later choose to let low-risk items, such as thank-you replies to five-star reviews, go out after a lighter check, but that is always your decision.

Can the review agent filter out unhappy customers?

No. The Review Request Agent asks every eligible customer the same way. It does not write reviews, buy reviews or screen customers by how satisfied they seem.

Do marketing agents guarantee rankings or more leads?

No. Agents handle consistent execution of marketing tasks. They do not set strategy and we never promise rankings, traffic or revenue.
